## The market view

The market will offer a convincing story every day. We do not need to trade every story. Our job is to own a sensible collection of assets, pay reasonable prices and leave enough room for mistakes. Patience is useful only when the underlying reasoning still holds.

## The process comes first

Keep 65% in a low-cost, diversified core and 35% in a handful of businesses you can explain. This model borrows broad principles associated with John Bogle, Benjamin Graham and Warren Buffett: control costs, demand a margin of safety and let sound businesses compound. None of them endorsed this portfolio. A good company can still be a poor investment at the wrong price. Before buying a satellite position, estimate a conservative value under several scenarios and insist on a meaningful discount. This dashboard does not establish that any stock is cheap today.

## Rebalance with purpose

Review once a year and whenever a holding moves more than five percentage points from target. Also review a direct stock above 8% or an estimated sector above 25%, even without a drift alert. Start with new contributions where possible. Consider taxes and transaction costs before selling. A broken investment thesis deserves attention even when the weight looks right; a falling price alone is not proof of a broken thesis.

## Mind your behavior

Write down why you own each position before the difficult day arrives. Keep near-term spending and an emergency reserve outside this model. Avoid panic selling, borrowed money and chasing last year's winners. Read business results more often than market headlines. If you cannot tolerate a deep equity drawdown, change the allocation thoughtfully before a crisis forces your hand.

## What could go wrong

This is still an 85% equity portfolio. A severe stock-market decline could produce substantial losses, and a 15% bond allocation may offer less protection than hoped. Inflation or rising rates can hurt both stocks and bonds. The satellites overlap with the US index; eight names are not eight independent bets. International markets bring currency and geopolitical risks. Businesses can deteriorate, management can misallocate capital and expensive shares can disappoint for years. There is no emerging-market allocation here. Approximate sector data can hide concentration. Diversification limits some risks; it cannot make investing safe.
