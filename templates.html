Module Overview and To Do List

Orient students to the topic, frame the learning, and provide a clear sequence of activities.

Make this template your own
  1. Copy the HTML and paste it into your Canvas page’s HTML editor.
  2. Replace every bracketed prompt with student-facing content.
  3. Use the hook to spark interest, the overview to orient students, and essential questions to guide inquiry. Organize the checklist in the actual learning sequence; add or remove activities to fit your module.
  4. Link exact course-item titles and map each activity to the objectives it actually supports. Define LO as Learning Objective on first use. Include due dates or link to the course schedule; check links and consistency with activity instructions before publishing.

Keep this overview concise; list individual tasks in the To Do List.]

Essential Questions

  • [Write a broad, open-ended question that frames the module’s central idea and aligns with its learning objectives. Invite interpretation, explanation, or judgment supported by evidence; avoid questions answered by a definition or a single fact.]
  • [Add a complementary question that asks students to transfer the central idea to a new situation, weigh competing perspectives, or examine its implications. Choose a question students can revisit and answer with greater depth as they learn.]

  5. Complete: [Link to the quiz, assignment, or project. Briefly identify the performance or product students will complete and direct them to the activity’s instructions and success criteria or rubric.] [LO: insert aligned objective numbers]

Learning Resources

Help students use readings, media, and tools to make sense of real situations and prepare for meaningful practice.

Design for application
  1. Choose resources that directly support the module objectives. Use the three resource blocks as examples; duplicate, adapt, or remove them to match your course.
  2. Replace each bracketed prompt with student-facing directions. Identify what to use, why it matters, and what students should do with it.
  3. Use Connect to Practice to move from a realistic situation to an action or decision supported by evidence. Scale the task to students’ current knowledge and provide support before asking for independent application.
  4. Specify whether responses are notes, preparation, or submissions. Keep these directions consistent with the module checklist and assessment instructions.
  5. Add actual resource links and verify access, captions, file accessibility, and any software requirements. The template deliberately includes link-placement instructions rather than nonworking links.

Assignment

Make the purpose, task, and criteria clear so students understand what to do, why it matters, and what successful work looks like.

Design a clear, aligned assignment
  1. Start with the learning objectives and the evidence students need to produce. Choose a meaningful task that allows them to demonstrate that learning.
  2. Replace every bracketed prompt with student-facing content. Adapt the five suggested steps to your task; combine, reorder, or remove steps that do not apply.
  3. Use the overview for the scope, the directions for the process, and grading criteria for the qualities of successful work. Keep requirements consistent across all three.
  4. Add real resource links and a usable rubric or scoring guide. Check file access, submission settings, points, dates, and any tool or collaboration expectations before publishing.

Directions

[Tell students exactly what to review and what to notice in those materials. Identify the first planning action, such as choosing a case, analyzing a prompt, gathering data, or outlining an approach. Link to any required template or example and explain how to use it.]

Step 2: Develop Your Work

[Describe how students should begin building the product or performing the task. Use observable action verbs, specify essential tools or methods, and explain any constraints that affect their choices. Include an achievable starting point for students who are new to the task.]

Strategy for Success:

[Offer one practical strategy for a likely difficulty or misconception. Explain how the strategy helps students make progress. Keep required instructions in the numbered steps so this tip does not introduce a hidden requirement.]

Working Expectations: [Clarify relevant boundaries for collaboration, sources, tools, or AI assistance in language consistent with your course policies. State any required acknowledgment or documentation. Remove categories that do not apply.]

Step 4: Explain and Support Your Decisions

[Specify the reasoning students need to make visible: for example, how evidence supports a conclusion, why a method fits the problem, or how a design serves its audience. State where this explanation belongs and its expected scope. If explanation is already integrated into the product, direct students to those components rather than requiring a duplicate response.]

Step 5: Review and Revise

[Ask students to compare their work with the published criteria and make revisions before submitting. Replace the checklist below with concrete checks that reflect this assignment; do not add requirements that appear nowhere else.]

  • [Check that every required component is present and addresses the task.]
  • [Check the accuracy of key ideas, calculations, procedures, or evidence using an appropriate method.]
  • [Check that decisions or conclusions are supported by the required reasoning.]
  • [Check that sources and any permitted assistance are acknowledged as directed.]
  • [Check that the intended audience can read, view, hear, or otherwise access the complete submission.]

Submission Instructions

[State exactly where and how students should submit their work. Identify accepted file types or response format, the number of files, and any necessary naming or sharing-permission requirements. Ensure these instructions match the Canvas assignment settings. Provide the due date and time with time zone, or clearly direct students to the assignment’s displayed due date.]

[Explain how students can confirm a successful submission and verify that the submitted file or link opens correctly. State any applicable resubmission expectations and where students can get help if they encounter a problem.]

Remember:

[Reinforce the most important quality of successful work in one encouraging sentence. Point back to the purpose or a key criterion, such as making reasoning visible or using evidence accurately. Avoid vague reminders such as “add detail,” and do not introduce a new requirement.]

Grading Criteria

[Explain how the work will be evaluated and tell students exactly where to find the rubric or scoring guide. Name the major criteria and their points or weights as appropriate. Describe observable qualities of successful work, align them with the stated objectives, and ensure the total matches the assignment settings. Attach the promised rubric or provide the criteria here before publishing.]

[Tell students where they will receive feedback and when they can expect it. If revision is part of the assignment, explain how they should use that feedback.]

Learning Resources Template 2

Keep playable media and reading guidance alongside application activities. Includes your IBM article and YouTube video as examples.

Prepare embedded learning resources
  1. Replace the bracketed guidance with student-facing directions. Adapt the objectives, preparation, and application prompts to your resources.
  2. The example YouTube video has an embedded player and a direct link. For another video, use the provider’s embed code, give the player a descriptive title, and keep a direct link below it.
  3. The IBM article cannot be embedded: its website restricts which sites may display it. This template shows a brief reading introduction on the page and links to the complete article. It does not reproduce or embed IBM’s full text.
  4. For another article, use a publisher-approved embed when available. If embedding is blocked, retain an introduction and direct link, or use an accessible reading you have permission to host in Canvas.
  5. After saving in Canvas, check the page in Student View: play the video, check captions or a transcript, test links and mobile sizing, and verify any article embed. A GitHub preview does not establish that your institution’s Canvas settings will permit every embed.
