Module Overview and To Do List

Orient students to the topic, frame the learning, and provide a clear sequence of activities.

Make this template your own
  1. Copy the HTML and paste it into your Canvas page’s HTML editor.
  2. Replace every bracketed prompt with student-facing content.
  3. Use the hook to spark interest, the overview to orient students, and essential questions to guide inquiry. Organize the checklist in the actual learning sequence; add or remove activities to fit your module.
  4. Link exact course-item titles and map each activity to the objectives it actually supports. Define LO as Learning Objective on first use. Include due dates or link to the course schedule; check links and consistency with activity instructions before publishing.

[Module Number: Concise, Student-Friendly Title]

[Create a brief hook that makes students want to learn more. In 2–3 sentences, place them in a realistic situation, introduce a compelling dilemma, describe an unexpected outcome, or challenge a familiar assumption. Make the stakes concrete by connecting the situation to their lives, goals, or future work. Leave a tension or possibility for the module to explore; save the guiding questions for Essential Questions below.]

Module Overview 📘

[Orient students to the learning ahead. Name the module’s central concepts or skills and explain what students will be able to do with them by the end. Briefly connect this learning to prior knowledge or the larger course, then describe how students will move from exploring ideas to practicing and demonstrating their learning. Keep this overview concise; list individual tasks in the To Do List.]

Essential Questions

  • [Write a broad, open-ended question that frames the module’s central idea and aligns with its learning objectives. Invite interpretation, explanation, or judgment supported by evidence; avoid questions answered by a definition or a single fact.]
  • [Add a complementary question that asks students to transfer the central idea to a new situation, weigh competing perspectives, or examine its implications. Choose a question students can revisit and answer with greater depth as they learn.]

Learning Resources

Help students use readings, media, and tools to make sense of real situations and prepare for meaningful practice.

Design for application
  1. Choose resources that directly support the module objectives. Use the three resource blocks as examples; duplicate, adapt, or remove them to match your course.
  2. Replace each bracketed prompt with student-facing directions. Identify what to use, why it matters, and what students should do with it.
  3. Use Connect to Practice to move from a realistic situation to an action or decision supported by evidence. Scale the task to students’ current knowledge and provide support before asking for independent application.
  4. Specify whether responses are notes, preparation, or submissions. Keep these directions consistent with the module checklist and assessment instructions.
  5. Add actual resource links and verify access, captions, file accessibility, and any software requirements. The template deliberately includes link-placement instructions rather than nonworking links.

[Module Topic]: Learning Resources

[Introduce a realistic situation in which this module’s knowledge or skills matter. In 1–2 sentences, show what someone needs to understand, decide, or do. Connect to the module’s opening hook without repeating it.]

[Explain how these resources prepare students to perform a specific task or make an informed decision. Identify the learning objective or objectives they support and the recommended order for using them.]

Resource 1: [Reading Title] 📘

[Identify the reading and author or source. Specify the required chapter, pages, or sections and whether the resource is required or optional. State what students should focus on and how it prepares them for the next learning activity.]

[Insert a descriptive link here using the Canvas editor, such as “Read: Resource Title.” Link to the actual reading rather than a general website.]

Connect to Practice

  • [Describe a brief, realistic situation in which the reading’s central concept helps explain a problem or inform a decision. Ask students to identify the relevant concept and connect it to specific details in the situation.]
  • [Ask students to recommend an action or explain a likely outcome using evidence from the reading. Specify the decision and its constraints so students have something concrete to reason about.]
  • [State how students should capture their thinking, such as a brief note or annotated example, and where they will use it next. Clarify whether it is preparation only or a required submission; link to submission instructions when applicable.]

Resource 2: [Video or Demonstration Title] 🎥

[Identify the video or demonstration and its creator. Specify the full length or assigned segment, required or optional status, and the process, decision, or technique students should observe. Provide access to captions or a transcript.]

[Insert a descriptive link here, such as “Watch: Video Title.” If you embed the video, also provide a direct link and check that captions work.]

Connect to Practice

  • [Identify a specific moment or step to pause at. Ask students to notice the evidence, cues, or constraints that influence the demonstrator’s action.]
  • [Present a similar situation with one meaningful change. Ask students to predict how the approach should change and explain their reasoning using what they observed.]
  • [Ask students to describe a step they would try themselves and how they would judge whether it worked. State whether this is a personal reflection, preparation for practice, or a response to submit.]

Resource 3: [Guide, Tool, or Worked Example Title] 📊

[Identify the guide, tool, or worked example and its purpose. State when students should use it, whether it is required or optional, and any file format or software needed. Explain how its organization helps students complete the target task.]

[Insert a descriptive link to the current resource, such as “Download: Guide Title (PDF).” For a course file, select the file within this Canvas course so the link points to the correct copy.]

Connect to Practice

  • [Provide a manageable, realistic case or sample input. Ask students to select the relevant part of the guide or tool and use it to complete a task or reach a decision.]
  • [Ask students to justify their result with the case evidence and the guide’s criteria. Include a way to check their work, such as a worked example, answer explanation, or feedback in the practice activity.]
  • [Introduce a limitation, ambiguous detail, or changed condition. Ask students what additional information or adjustment they would need before using the result in practice.]

Check Your Understanding ✅

[Turn the aligned learning objectives into 2–3 observable self-checks. Ask students to produce an explanation, decision, or demonstration rather than simply rate their confidence. Refer to the application prompts above and the work they will do next.]

  • [Explain a central concept in your own words and illustrate it with a new real-world example. Replace this prompt with the specific concept students must explain.]
  • [Apply the method or tool to a short, unfamiliar case and justify the result with evidence. Identify the case and the criteria students should use.]
  • [Compare two possible approaches or identify an error, then explain a correction. Specify the approaches or error so students can check a concrete skill.]

[Tell students how to check their responses and what to revisit if they need more practice. Link to the relevant explanation, feedback, or support, then identify the next activity. Do not imply that self-checks are graded unless they are.]
